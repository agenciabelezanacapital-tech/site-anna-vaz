Balayage vs. highlights vs. babylights: which blonde is right for you?

The three most requested blonde techniques in Greater Boston explained by colorist Anna Vaz: how each one looks, how it grows out, how long it takes and who it suits.

Anna Vaz Concept · Malden, MA · 2026-10-06

Every week someone sits in Anna Vaz's chair in Malden and says the same thing: “I want to be blonde, but I don't know if I want balayage or highlights.” The honest answer is that the technique is a tool, and the right tool depends on your hair, your routine and the picture in your head. Here is how Anna explains the difference to clients.

Highlights: the brightest, most even blonde

Traditional highlights are woven in sections and wrapped in foil. The foil traps heat, so the lightener lifts more, which is why highlights give the lightest, most uniform result. They start right at the root, which means a visible regrowth line after 8 to 12 weeks.

Best for: people who want to be noticeably blonde all over, fine or light-brown hair, and anyone who does not mind coming back every two to three months.

Babylights: highlights, but whisper-thin

Babylights use the same foil method with much finer sections, so the blonde blends into your base like a child's sun-bleached hair. The result is soft and expensive-looking, and it grows out more gracefully than chunky highlights.

Best for: natural-looking brightness, face-framing light, covering early grays softly, and clients who want “my hair, but better.”

Balayage: hand-painted, lived-in dimension

Balayage means “to sweep” in French. The colorist paints lightener onto the surface of the hair, usually starting away from the root and saturating the ends. There is no foil, so the lift is softer and the transition is seamless. That is why balayage grows out for 3 to 5 months without a line.

Best for: low-maintenance clients, brunettes who want a sun-kissed look, long hair, and anyone who wants dimension more than brightness.

A common misunderstanding: balayage is not a color. It is a placement technique. You can have a caramel balayage on dark hair (bronde) or a very light beige balayage on blonde hair.

Foilyage and teasylights: the hybrids

Boston salons increasingly offer hybrids. Foilyage is balayage placement wrapped in foil, so you get the soft, blended look with more lift. Teasylights backcomb each section before painting, which creates a diffused root. Both are great for dark bases that need extra lift without a harsh line.

Anna's shortcut: if you say “I want to be blonde”, you probably want highlights or babylights. If you say “I want my hair lighter but natural”, you want balayage. If you are dark and want to look like you just came back from the beach, that is bronde.

Frequently asked questions

Is balayage less damaging than highlights?

Usually yes, because there is no foil and the root is left natural, so less of the hair is lightened. But any lightening needs bond protection, which is standard in Anna's method.

Can I get balayage on dark brown or black hair?

Yes. On very dark hair, Anna usually suggests bronde or foilyage first so the lift is controlled and the hair stays healthy, then goes lighter over sessions if you want.

Which lasts longer?

Balayage. A lived-in balayage can go 3 to 5 months; highlights and babylights usually need a touch-up every 8 to 12 weeks.
